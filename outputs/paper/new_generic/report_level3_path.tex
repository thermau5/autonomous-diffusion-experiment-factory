\begin{abstract}
The generic certificate bounds excess terminal risk as
$Q = Q_0 + \tfrac{c_{\mathrm{stat}}}{n}\!\int\! \tfrac{a}{\rho}
      + c_{\mathrm{disc}}\!\int\! \tfrac{d}{m^{p}}$,
with optimal sampling density $m^\star\!\propto d^{1/(p+1)}$. Each design
choice enters through one term (Table~\ref{tab:axes}). Prior locked results
varied the schedule $m$ and solver $s$ on a fixed EDM path; here we test
every axis on a new model family, Rectified Flow (RF). Two headline findings:
(i) on the \textbf{path} axis, the reflow ladder 1/2/3-RF produces the
floor-vs-defect \emph{crossover cascade} the certificate predicts --- no
single path wins at all budgets; (ii) on the \textbf{schedule} axis, the
\emph{parameter-free} certificate-calibrated grid $m^\star$ wins or ties the
native default of every configuration tested --- four EDM solver cores and
four path families (EDM, RF, VP-SDE, OT-CFM) --- losing only at the lowest
budgets (EMS at NFE 5--8, OT-CFM at NFE 5). It is FID-optimal on 1-RF (a
free-node search cannot beat it), the only scheduler never catastrophic
against optimized baselines (tuned $\rho$, GITS, surrogate-DP;
Table~\ref{tab:schedaxis}), at $10$--$500\times$ less preparation compute, and
transfers to FFHQ-64 with the CIFAR exponents unchanged, beating the Karras
default at every budget. All FID is Clean-FID 10k, 3 seeds (FFHQ: EDM's FID
implementation, both arms).
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Results at a glance.}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item[$s$:] DPM-Solver-v3 (EMS) is the strongest solver core --- best floor
($\approx4.35$), winning at mid-to-high NFE on the fixed EDM path --- a
Level-1 advance.
\item[$\R$:] The directly-measurable feature-space $W_2$ risk tracks FID
in rank $5/5$ --- it is the FID-faithful risk.
\item[$P$:] Reflow trades the floor $Q_0$ for the defect $d$; the optimal
reflow count is \emph{NFE-dependent} (3-RF at NFE 5, 2-RF at 8--64, 1-RF at
$\ge128$). Pre-registered crossover prediction confirmed.
\item[$m$:] The calibrated node placement $m^\star\!\propto d^{1/(p+1)}$
($p\!=\!1$, no metric feedback) wins or ties the native default of every
solver core and path family tested, losing only at the lowest budgets (EMS
NFE 5--8, OT-CFM NFE 5; Table~\ref{tab:dominance}). It is FID-optimal on 1-RF,
the only optimized scheduler never catastrophic in any group
(Table~\ref{tab:schedaxis}), and transfers to FFHQ-64 with no re-tuning.
\end{itemize}

\section{Path axis $P$: the reflow ladder}
\label{sec:path}

\noindent\textbf{Reading the table.} Wins concentrate at low-to-mid NFE,
where node placement decides whether the high-curvature region is resolved;
as NFE grows every schedule converges to the same floor and the cells tie
($\int d/m^{p}\!\to\!0$). The pattern is uniform across all four solver cores
and all four path families, so $m^\star$ is not an EDM artifact. VP-SDE is the
cleanest case --- Euler never reaches its floor in range, so $m^\star$ wins at
every budget ($12$--$16\%$). The only losses are EMS at NFE 5--8 and OT-CFM at
NFE 5 (too few nodes for its peaked defect); OT-CFM also witnesses the path
axis, beating 1-RF at low NFE ($27.9$ vs $37.9$ at NFE 5) but with a higher
floor ($7.0$ vs $6.4$ at NFE 64) --- the same $Q_0$-vs-$d$ trade as the reflow
ladder.

\noindent Three findings. \emph{(i) Tuning the default's knob does not rescue
it.} The best per-NFE $\rho$ closes almost none of the low-NFE gap ($245$ vs
$m^\star$'s $46$ at NFE 5, $44$ vs $13$ at NFE 9): the failure is the
one-parameter family's shape, not the $\rho\!=\!7$ setting. DPM++ confirms it
--- the validation-best $\rho$ at NFE 5 \emph{is} the default $\rho\!=\!7$, and
tuned $\rho$ never beats $m^\star$ (a $0.02$ tie at NFE 64 aside).
\emph{(ii) GITS is budget-aware but core-fragile.} Its DP scores candidate
jumps at the actual step lengths, so it sees what the certificate's bound
cannot: curvature near the \emph{end} of a jump barely moves the endpoint
(lever $\int a(s)(t_{\mathrm{end}}-s)\,ds$; $38\%$ of 1-RF curvature mass sits
in $t\in[0.95,1]$ where the lever is $\le\!0.05$). Its edge over $m^\star$
grows with how peaked the defect is and how far Euler sits from its floor:
largest on VP-SDE (wins every budget, $189/98.7/54.9/34.3$ vs
$236/131/67.3/37.1$ at NFE 5--18), present at OT-CFM NFE 5 ($27.73$, the one
budget where $m^\star$ loses to the default), shrinking to a shared floor on
2-RF/3-RF. But it collapses on a second-order core: with the Heun jump cost,
GITS gives $242/57/18$ vs $m^\star$'s $46/13/8$ on EDM. $m^\star$ is the only
scheduler never catastrophic in any group.
\emph{(iii) The remaining headroom is quantified.} The free-node search bounds
what any scheduler can reach: $m^\star$ sits on that bound on 1-RF,
$0.7$--$1.3$ FID above it on EDM/Heun (NFE 9--13) and $0.6$--$1.6$ above on
EDM/DPM++ (NFE 8--12, $9.74\!\to\!8.17$, $6.07\!\to\!5.50$) --- and on both EDM
cores the optimum was found by refining the $m^\star$ grid itself.

\noindent $m^\star$ is the cheapest member of the cheapest access class

\emph{Relation to learned schedules (AYS).} Align-Your-Steps (Sabour et al.,
2024) optimizes schedules via a KLUB objective, but its CIFAR schedules are
unpublished and its FID protocol differs (50k legacy vs our 10k Clean-FID).
The free-node search is the stronger comparison on our protocol: it bounds
what any schedule optimizer could achieve, and $m^\star$ matches it without
optimization, FID feedback, or training.

\emph{Adaptive step-size control is not a substitute.} A Dormand--Prince RK45
solver on the same 1-RF ODE cannot operate below NFE ${\approx}19$ (6
evaluations per step), and at NFE 25 it is FID-blind --- FID is non-monotonic
in the tolerance ($12.0$ at tol $0.5$ vs $32.2$ at tol $0.1$). It crosses the
Euler frontier only at NFE ${\gtrsim}40$ ($5.29$ at NFE 83), where its
fifth-order core --- an $s$-axis advantage, not a scheduling one ---
dominates.

\noindent Two secondary findings (details in \S\ref{app:sched}):
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item\emph{Path\,$\times$\,schedule:} the calibrated schedule wins on 2-RF
and 3-RF too, but the gain shrinks as reflow pushes the model toward its
floor (less defect to recover) --- straightening and scheduling are
complementary, not redundant.
\item\emph{FID-faithful weighting fails:} the feature-weighted $d\!\cdot\!g$
is \emph{worse} than both pixel-$d$ and uniform; pixel-$d$ is sufficient.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
The certificate's structure holds on every axis we probe. Two claims transfer
off EDM: the \textbf{path} trades $Q_0$ for $d$ with an NFE-dependent optimum
(the confirmed crossover cascade), and the \textbf{parameter-free calibrated
schedule} wins or ties the native default of every solver core and path family
tested, losing only at the lowest budgets (EMS NFE 5--8, OT-CFM NFE 5), and
carries to FFHQ-64 with no re-tuning. Against optimized schedulers it is
FID-optimal on 1-RF, within $\sim\!1$ FID of optimal on EDM/Heun, and uniquely
never-catastrophic --- at orders of magnitude less compute and the weakest
access requirements. Path, schedule, and solver are complementary advances on
orthogonal axes of one certificate.
